Evolution of New Zealand Currency

From Imperial British coins to independent decimal money

Prior to Official NZ Coinage (1897–1933): Imperial British coins and Australian-minted sovereigns circulated as legal tender under the Imperial Coinage Act 1870.

Coinage Act 1933: Introduced New Zealand's first distinct national coinage (Halfpenny to Crown). British coinage ceased being legal tender on 1 February 1935.

Decimal Currency Day (10 July 1967): Conversion from £sd to dollars and cents (£1 = $2.00, 10/- = $1.00, 1/- = 10c, 1d = 5/6c).

Authority: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (RBNZ) Key Designers: Percy Metcalfe, George Kruger Gray, Leonard Mitchell, Robert Conly

Series 5 (Paper) vs. Series 6 (Polymer) Banknote Transition

Comparing substrate material, transparent security windows, and tactile ink innovations

Series 5: Issued from 1992 to 1999 (redesigned with native New Zealand flora, fauna, and historical figures on cotton paper).
Series 6: Issued from 1999 to 2014 (transitioned from paper to flexible plastic polymer material with transparent windows while maintaining identical portrait artwork).

Feature Series 5 (1992–1999) Series 6 (1999–2014)
Substrate Material Cotton-based banknote paper Flexible plastic polymer (Guardian substrate)
Transparent Windows None (solid opaque paper note) Two transparent windows (one oval, one fern-shaped)
Durability & Cleanliness Tore easily, absorbed dirt, and degraded when wet Waterproof, difficult to tear, and significantly cleaner
Embossing & Feel Standard paper intaglio printing Raised ink intaglio (enhanced tactile identification)
Watermark / Shadow Image Traditional watermark embedded in the paper A shadow image of Queen Elizabeth II next to the oval window

Crown Heads & Monarchical Effigies on NZ Currency

Historical record of royal portraits featured across circulating coins and Reserve Bank banknotes

King George V (1910–1936) 1933–1935 Coins

Sculptor: Percy Metcalfe.
Design: Crowned bust facing left wearing the Imperial Crown and the Robe of State with the Collar of the Most Honourable Order of the Bath. Featured on the introductory 1933, 1934, and 1935 silver series, plus the historic 1935 Waitangi Crown.

King George VI (1936–1952) 1940–1952 Coins & S2 Notes

Coins Designer: Thomas Humphrey Paget (T.H. Paget). Uncrowned left-facing portrait on all coins from 1940 to 1952.
Banknotes Designer: Bradbury Wilkinson & Co. Portrait of King George VI in naval uniform on all Series 2 banknotes (10/-, £1, £5, £10, £50) from 1940 to 1967.

Queen Elizabeth II — Coinage 1953–2026 Coins

1st Effigy (1953–1965): Mary Gillick (young laureate bust facing right with tie ribbon).

2nd Effigy (1967–1985): Arnold Machin (wearing the Girls of Great Britain & Ireland Tiara).

3rd Effigy (1986–1998): Raphael Maklouf (crowned with George IV State Diadem).

4th Effigy (1999–Present): Ian Rank-Broadley (mature portrait with tiara).

Queen Elizabeth II — Banknotes 1967–Present Notes

Series 3 & 4 (1967–1992): Anthony Buckley portrait of the Queen wearing the formal Robes, Collar, and Star of the Order of the Garter across all paper decimal denominations ($1 to $100).
Series 5, 6 & 7 (1992–Present): Robert Anderson portrait reserved exclusively on the $20 banknote.

King Charles III (2022–Present) 2027+ Coinage

Sculptor: Dan Thorne (Royal Canadian Mint).
Design: Official uncrowned effigy of King Charles III facing left (reversing the direction of Queen Elizabeth II in accordance with royal numismatic tradition). The 10-cent piece is scheduled as the first coin to enter general circulation around 2027.

King Tāwhiao (2nd Māori King) Series 1 Notes (1934–1940)

Banknote Engraver: Thomas de la Rue & Co.
Historical Significance: King Tāwhiao (Potatau Te Wherowhero II) was depicted wearing a huia feather headdress on New Zealand's first national banknotes (10/-, £1, £5, £50) in 1934, symbolising indigenous authority alongside the newly formed Reserve Bank.

Key Historical & Denomination Notes

Critical numismatic milestones, decimal changeovers, and coinage reforms

⏸️ 1966 Minting Gap Transition Year

No regular circulating coins were dated 1966 in order to deplete remaining pre-decimal stocks and prepare for Decimal Currency Day on 10 July 1967.

🚫 Phase-outs (1c, 2c & 5c) Demonetisation

1c & 2c coins ceased minting in 1988 and were demonetised on 30 April 1990. The 5c piece ceased minting after 2004 and was demonetised on 31 October 2006.

🥇 $1 & $2 Coin Introduction (1990) Banknote Replacement

In 1990, aluminium-bronze $1 (Kiwi) and $2 (Kōtuku) coins were minted to permanently replace the $1 and $2 paper banknotes, entering circulation on 11 February 1991.

⚡ 2006 Coinage Reform Surge Size & Alloy Reform

In July 2006, the 10c, 20c, and 50c coins were resized using plated steel, causing an unprecedented single-year surge of 327,000,000 replacement coins.
