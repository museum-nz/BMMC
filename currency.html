Evolution of New Zealand Currency

From Imperial British coins to independent decimal money

Prior to Official NZ (1897–1933): In 1897, New Zealand's coins became subject to parts of the Imperial Coinage Act 1870 (UK). This meant only British coin was the official legal tender in the colony, alongside Australian-minted gold sovereign and half-sovereign coins.

Coin Smuggling & The 1933 Act: By 1933, widespread coin smuggling and severe shortages of lower-value coins forced action. Instead of immediate decimalisation (as advocated by the New Zealand Numismatic Society), New Zealand introduced its own distinctive national coinage under the Coinage Act 1933. British coin officially ceased being legal tender on 1 February 1935.

Decimal Currency Day (10 July 1967): New Zealand introduced dollars and cents (£1 = $2.00, 10s = $1.00, 1s = 10c, 1d = 5/6c).

Authority: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (RBNZ) Key Designers: Percy Metcalfe, George Kruger Gray, Leonard Mitchell, Robert Conly
