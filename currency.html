Evolution of New Zealand Currency

From Imperial British coins to independent decimal money

Prior to Official NZ Coinage (1897–1933): Imperial British coins and Australian-minted sovereigns circulated as legal tender under the Imperial Coinage Act 1870.

Coinage Act 1933: Introduced New Zealand's first distinct national coinage (Halfpenny to Crown). British coinage ceased being legal tender on 1 February 1935.

Decimal Currency Day (10 July 1967): Conversion from £sd to dollars and cents (£1 = $2.00, 10/- = $1.00, 1/- = 10c, 1d = 5/6c).

Authority: Reserve Bank of New Zealand (RBNZ) Key Designers: Percy Metcalfe, George Kruger Gray, Leonard Mitchell, Robert Conly

Key Historical & Denomination Notes

Critical numismatic milestones, decimal changeovers, and coinage reforms

⏸️ 1966 Minting Gap Transition Year

No regular circulating coins were dated 1966 in order to deplete remaining pre-decimal stocks and prepare for Decimal Currency Day on 10 July 1967.

🚫 Phase-outs (1c, 2c & 5c) Demonetisation

1c & 2c coins ceased minting in 1988 and were demonetised on 30 April 1990. The 5c piece ceased minting after 2004 and was demonetised on 31 October 2006 under Swedish rounding rules.

🥇 $1 & $2 Coin Introduction (1990) Banknote Replacement

In 1990, aluminium-bronze $1 (Kiwi) and $2 (Kōtuku) coins were minted to permanently replace the $1 (brown/fantail) and $2 (purple/rifleman) paper banknotes, officially entering general circulation on 11 February 1991.

⚡ 2006 Coinage Reform Surge Size & Alloy Reform

In July 2006, the 10c, 20c, and 50c coins were resized (made significantly smaller and lighter using copper- and nickel-plated steel), causing an unprecedented single-year surge of 327,000,000 newly minted replacement coins.
