Artifact Care, Cleaning & Surface Restoration

Time-tested workshop techniques, safe chemical recipes, and preventative maintenance strategies for metals, fine hardwoods, leather, and heritage finishes.

Jump to: ⚠️ Safety & Patina 🪙 Metals (Silver, Brass, Iron) 🪵 Hardwood & Finishes 📜 Leather, Pewter & Cookware
⚠️

Workshop Safety & Fire Prevention

Essential PPE and chemical precautions

  • • Ventilation & PPE: Always work in well-ventilated areas when using Mineral Turpentine, Kerosene, or Methylated Spirits. Use chemical-resistant nitrile gloves and eye protection.
  • • Spontaneous Combustion Hazard: Rags saturated with oils, turpentine, or solvents can spontaneously ignite as they dry. Seal used rags in an airtight, water-filled metal container before disposal.
  • • Wax Heating Rule: Never heat beeswax or turpentine over an open flame. Always use an electric hotplate on the lowest setting or an electric double boiler.
🔍

The Golden Rule: Preserve Historic Patina

Distinguishing harmful oxidation from valuable patina

Before applying acidic baths or metal polishes, assess whether the item is a rare collectible. Original aged patina on historical bronze, brass, coin silver, and antique iron accounts for a major portion of its historical significance and monetary value.

Museum Advice: Clean away loose grit and corrosive surface salts, but avoid stripping metal back to raw, bright factory finish unless required for functional restoration.

Metal Cleaning & Preservation

Gentle electrochemical dips, natural formulas, and rust conversion

✨ Silver (Electrochemical Dip)

Non-Abrasive

Cleans deep tarnish through ionic transfer in seconds without rubbing away microscopic silver layers.

Solution Formula: 1 rounded dessertspoon of Washing Soda Crystals (e.g. Greenwoods) per 1 Litre of hot water.
Method: Line a plastic or aluminium basin with aluminium foil. Submerge the item, ensuring it maintains direct contact with the aluminium. Light tarnish clears in seconds. For stubborn crevices, gently agitate with a soft paintbrush. Rinse in clean water and buff dry.
⚠️ Never submerge: Jewelry with mounted pearls, coral, turquoise, opals, glued parts, or wooden/bone cutlery handles.

🎺 Brass & Copper

Natural Acid

Removes green verdigris, grease, and heavy atmospheric oxidation using kitchen-grade pantry solutions.

1. Surface Tarnish (Rubbing Paste): Mix 2 tbsp fine salt and 2 tbsp white vinegar into a paste. Apply using a soft cotton cloth, rub gently along the contour, rinse thoroughly, and dry immediately.
2. Heavy Encrustation (Simmer Method): Immerse tarnished solid copper or brass into a pot of water with the salt & vinegar mixture. Bring to a gentle boil until grime releases. Let cool, wash with warm soapy water, rinse, and buff to a warm luster.

🔩 Iron & Rust Derusting

De-Oxidation

Gentle citric conversion bath for heritage iron tools, machine castings, fittings, and brackets.

Mild Citric Bath: Dissolve 1 tbsp Citric Acid crystals per 10 L of water. Soak items until rust is loosened. Remove heavy scale with a soft wire brush first.
Rinse & Immediate Protection: Wash down with clean water, dry immediately with a clean towel or heat gun to prevent flash rust. Coat decorative iron with Liberon Iron Paste or CRC Zinc Rust Protection.

Hardwood Furniture & Cabinet Care

Environmental defenses, homemade archival wax, and spot repairs

🌡️ Environmental Threats to Avoid

  • Thermal & Humidity Shock: Hot, dry air causes shrinkage, joint failure, and cracking. Cold, damp air causes swelling, binding drawers, and warping.
  • Direct UV Sunlight: Bleaches natural timber pigmentation, dries wood grain, and degrades French polish and lacquer.
  • Mould & Rot Fungi: Fungi thrive in still, damp environments. Ensure continuous gentle air circulation.
  • House Borer (Common in NZ): Common wood-boring beetle causing flight holes and structural powdering.

🧽 Routine Maintenance & Strict DO NOTs

  • Dusting: Dust regularly with a lightly dampened cloth, followed immediately by buffing with a soft dry cloth. Dust unfinished wood with a completely dry cloth only.
  • Wax Application: Buff with hard wood paste wax or microcrystalline wax (Museum wax) once yearly (quarterly for dining tables). Buff horizontally, then vertically.
  • 🚫 NEVER USE RED OIL POLISH: Polishing oils attract atmospheric dust, oxidise, harden into a sticky film, and irreversibly blacken the wood grain.
  • 🚫 Avoid Unnecessary Chemical Stripping: Harsh stripping destroys natural patina and original stain depths.
🐝 Non-Perishable Archival Formula

Homemade Hardwood Wax Paste

A resilient, inert paste that forms a crystal-clear, mould-resistant protective shell over all wood types.

Base 1 kg Refined Beeswax
Solvent 500 ml Mineral Turpentine
Hardener 2 tbsp Carnauba Wax
Preparation: Gently heat ingredients together on an electric hotplate on the lowest setting (no open flames!) until thoroughly melted and uniform (soft butter consistency). Pour into clean glass jars to cool and seal.

🩺 Quick Wood Defect Repair Matrix

Condition / Issue Underlying Cause Workshop Remedy
White Spots / Rings Moisture trapped in varnish layer Gently polish with 0000 steel wool lubricated with a mix of mineral turpentine & oil (cooking or baby oil). If spot persists, moisture penetrated bare wood and localized refinishing is required.
Blushing / White Haze Trapped humidity in lacquer/varnish Lightly rub the entire surface with 0000 steel wool and mineral turpentine. Wipe clean, let dry, and apply a fresh coat of hard wax.
Dents (Softwoods / Pine) Crushed wood fibers Place a damp cotton cloth over the dent and hover a hot clothes iron over it to produce steam. The steam swells compressed fibers back into place. Never allow the iron to touch dry wood.
Crazing / Fine Cracks Aged, embrittled spirit varnish Re-dissolve and level the surface by carefully brushing methylated spirits/alcohol over the crack until it softens. Let cure and re-wax.
Chips & Deep Scratches Physical impact Fill void using a color-matched wax or shellac burn-in stick, level flush with a razor blade, and buff.
Heavy Wax / Dirt Grime Accumulated grime & mould Wipe with pure mineral turpentine on a lint-free cloth. Cuts through grime and mould build-up without dissolving the underlying shellac or varnish.

🪲 House Borer & Woodworm Eradication (NZ Protocol)

House borer flight holes indicate active larvae feeding inside timber cores.

Small Objects: Thermal Freeze Cycle

Seal small carvings, wooden boxes, or frames inside an airtight plastic bag and place into a domestic deep freezer for 7 to 10 days. Kills larvae and eggs without chemicals.

Large Furniture: Kerosene Syringe Injection

Using a fine medical syringe, inject mineral kerosene directly into fresh flight holes. Kerosene penetrates the tunnels and kills larvae while leaving an odour barrier that deters future re-infestation.

Specialist Materials: Leather, Pewter & Cookware

Preserving organic desk inlays, soft alloys, and distinguishing cookware from decorative cast iron

🛋️

Antique Leather

Desk tops & Chair upholstery: Dust with a dry microfibre cloth. Treat twice yearly with high-grade leather dressing containing lanolin and beeswax. Never use silicone sprays or dish detergents, which strip natural tanning oils and cause "red rot" powdering.

🫖

Pewter & Soft Alloys

Avoid acidic dips: Pewter contains tin and lead. Never use vinegar, lemon juice, or harsh abrasives on pewter as they cause permanent etching. Wash with warm water and mild soap, then polish gently with a paste of whiting powder and methylated spirits.

🍳

Cookware vs Decorative Iron

Vital Distinction: Chemical rust preventatives (Liberon paste, CRC zinc sprays) are for ornamental iron only. Historical cast iron cookware must be derusted via vinegar/electrolysis and seasoned exclusively with food-grade polymerized oils (flaxseed or grapeseed).
