Kinematic Principle

Why the Trailing Forward-Offset Geometry Self-Wedges

1 · Rest Tangent (~18.7°)

Zero-Bite Contact Geometry

With the pivot at (25.0, 462.5) and a 75.0 mm arm, exact tangency (353.0 + 41.5 = 394.5 mm) occurs at θ = 18.66° forward of vertical. A light extension spring on the Ø3.2 anchor holes holds the roller gently on the tire crown.

2 · Self-Wedging (< 18.7°)

Torque-Driven Compression

Driving the wheel forward (+X) creates a rearward (-X) traction reaction on the roller. Acting ~72 mm below the pivot, this pulls the arm clockwise toward smaller θ, shortening the wheel-to-roller center distance and multiplying normal grip under load.

3 · M5 Wedge-Lock (~13.7°)

Controlled 2 mm Bite Limit

At wedge = rest − 5° (~13.7°), the 2 mm flat stop pad on the arm’s rear neck (28 mm from pivot) kisses the M5 bolt in its ±3 mm fore-aft slot. Excess wedging torque transfers into the U-bracket legs rather than over-crushing the tire.

4 · Magnetic Park (~58°)

Rear-Catch Flush Latch

Swinging the arm forward to ~58° lifts the roller 37.5 mm clear of the tire while rotating the R19 pivot head back against twin Ø15×1.5 mm neodymium magnets mounted flush on the U-bracket rear-catch shelf.
